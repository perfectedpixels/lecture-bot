Build a low-fidelity paper prototype of your proposed product that demonstrates two end-to-end use cases drawn directly from your Customer Journey Map.

This assignment tests whether your concept holds together as a coherent system, not a collection of isolated screens. You should think holistically about how actions in one part of the experience inform, enable, or enhance other parts of the product. 

Keep the solution simple, intentional, and connected. Be holistic in your approach and above all else, keep it simple!

Fidelity Expectations

  • Prototypes should be rough and clearly low-fidelity (paper, whiteboard, or sketch-based).
  • Use placeholders for content, but include enough detail to communicate:
    • Core functionality
    • Available actions and choices
    • System responses and transitions between steps
  • You are not being evaluated on visual polish or drawing skill.
  • Fidelity should be placeholder rough, but enough to represent the functionality for each screen and the content and options available per step. 
    • Screens of the proposed experience as either snapshots of your whiteboard sketches / paper-based low fidelity mockups dropped into PowerPoint, Keynote, Google Slides, PDF, or Figma (but not designed in them!). You can also download a printable template and draw using those.

Required Components

Your submission must include:

  1. Two complete, end-to-end use cases

    • Each use case should map directly to a defined user goal or need from your Customer Journey

    • Show all screens required to start, progress through, and complete each use case

  2. Main navigation

    • Clearly indicate the primary navigation model and how users orient themselves within the system

  3. Screen-by-screen flow

    • Include every necessary step (no gaps or implied jumps)

    • Demonstrate how system state or prior actions affect later screens

  4. Brief explanation per use case

    • What the user is trying to accomplish

    • Why the proposed features are valuable to this persona

    • How the flow supports your Product Objective

  5. Persona and objective alignment

    • Explicitly connect design decisions back to:

      • Your primary persona(s)

      • Your product vision / Working Backwards Pitch

  6. Original thinking

    • Avoid simply mirroring existing apps or familiar screen-to-screen patterns

    • Look for distinctive interactions, shortcuts, or system intelligence that reflect your concept

  7. Engagement strategy

    • Show how the experience encourages continued use, trust, or momentum for your persona

Rubric

Criteria 4.0 – Excellent 3.0 – Good 2.0 – Marginal 1.0 – Unsatisfactory
Use Case Completion Two fully end-to-end use cases with all required screens and clear outcomes Mostly complete use cases with minor gaps Incomplete or uneven use cases Fragmented or unclear flows
Persona & Product Alignment Strong, explicit alignment to persona needs and Product Objective General alignment with limited justification Weak or inconsistent alignment No clear alignment
System Thinking Experience functions as a connected system; actions inform other areas Some interrelated behavior present Screens largely isolated No system logic
Flow & Navigation Clear, intuitive navigation and screen progression Mostly clear with minor confusion Navigation unclear or inconsistent Navigation absent or unusable
User Engagement & Value Thoughtful engagement strategies clearly add user value Some engagement consideration Minimal or generic engagement No engagement consideration
Explanation & Rationale Clear, concise explanations tied to goals and decisions Adequate explanation Vague or descriptive only Little or no explanation
