Translate your indexed UX frameworks into clickable, low-fidelity digital wireframes that operationalize your product concept.

Your wireframes should demonstrate how your persona, user goals, pain points, and Working Backwards Pitch come together as a coherent interaction system. This is the point in the project where your abstract thinking (frameworks, journeys, and prototypes) is stress-tested through real interaction flow. Your wireframes should not simply recreate your paper prototype. Your wireframes should demonstrate how an AI-assisted or agent-mediated system supports user goals over time—through guidance, automation, recommendations, or decision support—rather than relying solely on manual user input.

Requirements

1. Screen Coverage (Framework Alignment)

  • Include all screens from your paper prototype, plus any necessary intermediary screens or sub-steps required to complete your defined user goals.
  • Each primary use case must be completable end-to-end.
  • Screens and flows should clearly map back to:
    • Persona needs
    • Identified pain points
    • Product objective

2. Clickable Interaction (From Intent to Action)

  • All controls required to complete the happy paths must be clickable (buttons, links, selections, confirmations).
  • Not every screen must be interactive, but there must be clear click-through flows that demonstrate:
    • Decision points
    • Progression
    • Completion
  • Focus on primary flows, not edge cases.

    • Demonstrate where the AI agent exists in the experience

    • What the agent does vs. what the user does

    • How the agent meaningfully reduces effort, friction, or cognitive load

  • If the agent were removed, the experience should clearly degrade.

3. Fidelity & Constraint

  • Maintain low-fidelity wireframes:

    • No visual polish, branding, or final UI design
    • Use placeholders, basic shapes, and simple labels

  • This stage prioritizes:

    • Information hierarchy
    • Interaction logic
    • System consistency

Higher fidelity will come later. Over-design at this stage will work against you.

4. System Thinking & Improvement

Compared to your paper prototype, your wireframes should demonstrate improvement in:

  • Flow efficiency
  • Structural clarity
  • Reduction of unnecessary steps
  • Consistency across navigation, layout, and controls

This is where we assess whether your system thinking is holding up.

Tools

  • Wireframing must be completed in Figma Make or Google Stitch
  • Use of any other tool requires explicit instructor approval.
  • All screens must live in a single, organized file.

Usability & Framework Tenets

Your wireframes should demonstrate the following class tenets, derived from our shared UX frameworks:

  • Easy Discoverability
    Users can immediately understand where to start.

  • Quick Findability
    Key actions and information are surfaced without cognitive searching.

  • Cognitive Recognition
    Labels, patterns, and interactions align with established user mental models.

  • Continuity
    Screens connect logically across time, state, and task progression.

  • Intuitive Simplicity
    The experience is as simple as possible while still accomplishing the user goal.

  • Natural Feedback
    User actions produce clear, expected responses.

These tenets should be visible in the wireframes themselves—not just described.

In-Class Walkthrough

  • You will present a 10-minute walkthrough of your wireframes.
  • Be prepared to:
    • Walk through your primary use cases
    • Explain how key screens map back to your frameworks
    • Call out where and why the wireframes improve upon your paper prototype

Rubric
4.0 — Excellent 3.0 — Good 2.0 — Marginal 1.0 — Unsatisfactory
Wireframes clearly operationalize the indexed UX frameworks and product objective; the AI agent plays a meaningful, non-decorative role that reduces user effort or improves continuity; primary use cases are fully clickable end-to-end (including clear human ↔ agent interactions); the system is consistent and intuitive; class tenets (discoverability, findability, recognition, continuity, simplicity, feedback) are strongly embodied; and fidelity is appropriately low with clear improvement over the paper prototype. Framework alignment and agent presence are evident but uneven; core flows are mostly clickable with minor gaps; the system is generally understandable but has small inconsistencies; tenets are addressed but not fully reinforced; fidelity is mostly appropriate. Partial or superficial application of frameworks and AI agent value; limited or confusing click-through flows; noticeable inconsistencies; weak embodiment of UX tenets; fidelity choices distract from interaction logic. Minimal alignment to frameworks; unclear or absent AI agent value; nonfunctional or missing click-through flows; poor system coherence; tenets largely unmet; fidelity obscures intent.
