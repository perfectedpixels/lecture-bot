This assignment asks you to use your existing UX frameworks to map an end-to-end customer experience, with clear attention to how AI assists the user at key moments. Using your approved persona(s), Working Backwards Pitch, and documented user goals and pain points, you will create a Customer Journey Map and a Product Experience Storyboard that show how your product—and its AI-assisted features—improves the experience over time.

This is not an exercise in starting from scratch. Your work should build directly on artifacts you have already created and test whether your product concept holds up when mapped across real user touchpoints, decisions, and emotional states. You should be explicit about where AI appears in the experience, what it helps the user do, and where the user remains in control.

As a group, begin by revisiting the following required inputs:

  • Your primary persona(s) (from Assignment 2)

  • Your Working Backwards Pitch / Product Vision

  • Documented user goals, motivations, and pain points

  • Any assumptions or open questions identified in earlier assignments

Using these inputs, map all customer touchpoints and decision points before, during, and after the UX you plan to create. For each touchpoint, document the Pain Point, Opportunity, and Solution, noting when AI supports the user through guidance, recommendations, or automation.

Follow the steps taken in our in-class exercise. For more background on User Journeys, read: https://medium.com/choice-hacking/the-beginners-guide-to-creating-customer-journey-maps-69b62f6a41ef and watch Simon Sinek's How great leaders inspire action

  1. Discuss as a group your product and your product vision document. Outline the market the product would be servicing and the scenarios your user might use or might find a need in interacting with it. List all open questions or needs that are missing from the original proposal. 
  2. Based on that list, write down all the steps, online and offline, a user could possibly take to interact with your product and reach their final goal. Make sure to include any important steps taken even prior to using the actual product, or perhaps the decision process for when using your product would become valuable to them. 
  3. Group like-steps together into overarching stages and provide titles. 
  4. For each stage, brainstorm all services, features, and functionality that can enhance, support or fulfill the need.  
  5. Vote to determine the most important brainstorm ideas that are best serving each stage. Use Miro or FigJam to ideate and vote. Each contributor should cast their top 5 picks (e.g., with a ❤️) followed by their Second 5 (e.g., with a 👍) for 10 altogether. Use this Miro link for an example
  6. Draw a rough customer journey as it occurs online and offline for the end-to-end customer experience, mapping out the user journey you created and include captions plus list the top voted features for each frame. This should be demonstrated as a Customer Journey Map with an accompanying rough Storyboard. Use this tool to easily build with UXPressia  https://uxpressia.com/customer-journey-map-online-tool.
  7. From that, create a storyboard to demonstrate the improvement on the existing experience with your product as the solution to those gaps in the customer journey. Story boards should be at least 8 panels. They can be hand-drawn, or you are also free to assist in the creation of these materials using Generative AI. Photograph (if done analog) or screenshot each stage of the process. 
  8. The storyboard should start contain:
    • Where the problem starts for your user
    • How a user is introduced to the product service
    • How the experience solves the problem(s)
    • What are the outcomes based on it being solved

  • Storyboards may be:

    • Hand-drawn (photographed)
    • Digitally created
    • Assisted by Generative AI (with clear intent and structure)

 

Consider for each step: 

Context – Where is the user? What is around them? Are there any external factors which may be distracting them?  

Progression – How does each step enable them to get to the next? 

Devices – what device are they using?Are they a novice or expert? What features does the device have? 

Functionality – What type of functionality are they expecting? Is it achievable? 

Empathy mapping– What is their emotional state in each step? Are they engaged, bored, frustrated?
