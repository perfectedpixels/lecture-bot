COMMLD 515 (Levine) Advanced User Design


Summary  Agentic AI Advanced UX Design Studio is a graduate-level, studio-based course focused on designing next-generation human–AI experiences where intelligent agents do not merely respond, but reason, act, adapt, and collaborate on behalf of users.  Building on established UX foundations—user research, interaction design, usability engineering, and design systems—this course pushes students into the emerging discipline of agentic experience design. Students learn how to design AI agents as experience actors: entities with goals, perception, memory, autonomy, and behavioral constraints that must earn user trust while operating across complex workflows.  Rather than treating AI as a feature or interface layer, the course frames AI agents as participants in the experience ecosystem, requiring new UX artifacts, evaluation criteria, and ethical considerations. Students design systems where humans shift from direct control to intent-setting, oversight, and collaboration, reflecting real-world transitions already underway in industry.  Learning Objectives By the end of the course, students will be able to:  Design agentic UX systems using contemporary AI frameworks (Sense–Plan–Act–Reflect, progressive autonomy models, multi-agent coordination) Translate human goals into agent behaviors, guardrails, and interaction contracts Create agent personas that extend beyond demographics to include reasoning style, autonomy boundaries, memory models, and escalation logic Map human–agent journeys that account for trust, handoff, failure states, and long-term adaptation Prototype working agentic experiences that demonstrate real task execution, not just conversational output Critically evaluate AI systems for usability, reliability, explainability, and ethical risk Articulate design decisions using UX theory, AI capability constraints, and user evidence—at portfolio and industry-ready depth Studio Structure & Pedagogy This course follows the same studio-first instructional model used across your UX curriculum, adapted for advanced AI systems:  Short lecture blocks introduce theory, frameworks, and real-world case studies Guided studio work (2+ hours per session) focuses on hands-on design, critique, and iteration Lab time extends studio for prototyping, testing, and refinement Small-group teams mirror real product design teams working with AI engineers and stakeholders In-class critiques emphasize reasoning, tradeoffs, and system behavior—not visual polish alone Guest speakers from AI, UX, and product leadership provide industry grounding Because agentic UX relies heavily on collaboration, critique, and real-time iteration, in-person participation is required.  Core Frameworks The course is grounded in two primary sources that will be referenced continuously throughout lectures, studios, and critiques:  Agentic AI progression & systems thinking  Generative AI → Agentic workflows → Semi-autonomous systems Human-in-the-loop → Human-on-the-loop → Governance-driven oversight Single-agent vs. multi-agent orchestration Memory, reasoning, action, and failure containment Advanced UX evaluation frameworks, adapted for AI systems  Human-centered design principles under autonomy Trust, explainability, and error recovery as UX problems Heuristic evaluation for agent behavior (not just interfaces) UX writing, tone, and feedback loops for AI-mediated actions These frameworks will later be embedded directly into assignment rubrics, critique prompts, and grading language.  Major Studio Outcomes Across the quarter, student teams will:  Identify a real-world problem space where agentic AI meaningfully augments human work Conduct user research focused on intent, trust boundaries, and task delegation Build a full customer journey map including human–agent handoffs Design agent personas and behavioral models (goals, constraints, escalation rules) Prototype a working agentic experience (low- or high-fidelity, but behaviorally real) Test and iterate with users, focusing on usability, reliability, and trust Produce a final design proposal suitable for portfolio and stakeholder review Deliverables emphasize clarity of reasoning, evidence-based design decisions, and system thinking, not surface-level AI novelty.  The course structure includes a short lecture with small and large group activities, a two-hour studio for hands-on work on the projects. The lab is an extension of studio time. Guest speakers from the UX design field are planned throughout the quarter. Class time is intended to be highly collaborative and participation driven, making in-person required for students.  Example of a final Project:  Dia Life Trip Pal Links to an external site.
