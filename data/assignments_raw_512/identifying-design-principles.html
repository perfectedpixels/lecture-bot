Purpose

This assignment develops your ability to see and articulate aesthetic quality in digital interfaces.
You will examine how effective interfaces use visual composition, brand expression, and aesthetic harmony to create clarity, coherence, and emotional resonance.
Unlike Assignment 1, which focused on usability and user needs, this assignment centers entirely on form, visual decision-making, and interface craftsmanship—the “look and feel” that communicates quality and brand credibility.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment, you will be able to:

  1. Analyze interfaces using core aesthetic and compositional principles rather than usability metrics.

  2. Apply design vocabulary (layout, hierarchy, rhythm, proportion, balance, contrast, harmony) to describe visual systems precisely.

  3. Evaluate brand and visual consistency across interface elements.

  4. Reference theoretical frameworks such as Dieter Rams’ Ten Principles of Good Design, Gestalt principles, and neuroaesthetic clarity to justify your evaluations.

Assignment Instructions

Part 1 — Strong Design Aesthetics

  • Identify three (3) professional websites that exemplify high-quality interface aesthetics and brand coherence.

  • For each site, provide five (5) concrete visual examples (15 total).

  • Each example must include a screenshot and a short analytical explanation (2–3 sentences) describing how specific visual choices achieve quality.

Part 2 — Weak Design Aesthetics

  • Select two (2) professional websites that fail to demonstrate strong aesthetic principles.

  • Identify four (4) examples per site (8 total) with screenshots and analytic commentary.

  • Describe why each violates aesthetic quality—e.g., visual clutter, inconsistent alignment, poor contrast, off-brand typography, overuse of gradients, or conflicting imagery.

Maintain an objective, formal tone grounded in visual design terminology (avoid “I dislike this”; use “The lack of visual hierarchy flattens the composition and diminishes readability.”).

Evaluation Framework

Focus your observations on:

  • Composition & Balance: How elements are distributed; visual rhythm and alignment.

  • Hierarchy: How scale, contrast, and spatial grouping guide perception.

  • Color Theory: How hue, saturation, and value create mood or emphasize brand voice.

  • Typography: How font choice, weight, and spacing express tone and readability.

  • Imagery & Iconography: Cohesion of visual language and photographic/illustrative style.

  • Brand Consistency: Repetition of forms, color, and tone that reinforce identity.

  • White Space & Proportion: How breathing room supports visual clarity and focus.

Use the following principles (drawn from lectures and readings) to guide analysis:

  1. Balance & Harmony – Visual weight and rhythm create stability.

  2. Hierarchy & Emphasis – Relative size, color, and contrast establish order.

  3. Proportion & Scale – Relationships between elements feel intentional and pleasing.

  4. Unity & Consistency – Shared visual language builds recognition and trust.

  5. Contrast & Variety – Differentiation adds energy without chaos.

  6. White Space & Simplicity – Restraint communicates confidence.

  7. Color Theory & Emotion – Palette selection evokes appropriate affect.

  8. Typography & Tone – Type communicates hierarchy, voice, and legibility.

  9. Brand Expression – Every aesthetic decision aligns with identity and purpose.

Making sure you are considering from our lecture, the principles of good design:

  • Appealing through a positive experience (there is clarity of purpose and the elements of the design delight)
  • Consistency of connection to its brand (elements support brand system)
  • Follows the rules behind neuroesthetics (lack of clutter, easy to understand, pleasing use of imagery and color)
  • Using Gestalt principles (the eye is guided to core messages or calls to action)
  • Good use of white space (shows a focus of content or action by providing enough space around it)
  • Appropriate and compelling content (elements of the page drive towards user goals and imagery evokes a positive association with the brand)
  • Deft use of Size, Contrast, and Position (various content or calls to action have a hierarchy to their importance and are not competing for attention)
  • Color Theory (utilizing to evoke an emotion value with your audience)
