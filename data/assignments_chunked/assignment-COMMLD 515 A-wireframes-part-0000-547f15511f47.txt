Wireframes Translate your indexed UX frameworks into clickable, low-fidelity digital wireframes that operationalize your product concept. Your wireframes should demonstrate how your persona, user goals, pain points, and Working Backwards Pitch come together as a coherent interaction system. This is the point in the project where your abstract thinking (frameworks, journeys, and prototypes) is stress-tested through real interaction flow. Your wireframes should not simply recreate your paper prototype. Your wireframes should demonstrate how an AI-assisted or agent-mediated system supports user goals over time—through guidance, automation, recommendations, or decision support—rather than relying solely on manual user input. Requirements 1. Screen Coverage (Framework Alignment) Include all screens from your paper prototype, plus any necessary intermediary screens or sub-steps required to complete your defined user goals. Each primary use case must be completable end-to-end. Screens and flows should clearly map back to: Persona needs Identified pain points Product objective 2. Clickable Interaction (From Intent to Action) All controls required to complete the happy paths must be clickable (buttons, links, selections, confirmations). Not every screen must be interactive, but there must be clear click-through flows that demonstrate: Decision points Progression Completion Focus on primary flows, not edge cases. Demonstrate where the AI agent exists in the experience What the agent does vs. what the user does How the agent meaningfully reduces effort, friction, or cognitive load If the agent were removed, the experience should clearly degrade. 3. Fidelity & Constraint Maintain low-fidelity wireframes: No visual polish, branding, or final UI design Use placeholders, basic shapes, and simple labels This stage prioritizes: Information hierarchy Interaction logic System consistency Higher fidelity will come later. Over-design at this stage will work against you. 4. System Thinking & Improvement Compared to your paper prototype, your wireframes should demonstrate improvement in: Flow efficiency Structural clarity Reduction of unnecessary steps Consistency across navigation, layout, and controls This is where we assess whether your system thinking is holding up. Tools Wireframing must be completed in Figma Make or Google Stitch Use of any other tool requires explicit instructor approval. All screens must live in a single, organized file. Usability & Framework Tenets Your wireframes should demonstrate the following class tenets, derived from our shared UX frameworks: Easy Discoverability Users can immediately understand where to start. Quick Findability Key actions and information are surfaced without cognitive searching. Cognitive Recognition Labels, patterns, and interactions align with established user mental models.
