Identifying Design Principles Purpose This assignment develops your ability to see and articulate aesthetic quality in digital interfaces. You will examine how effective interfaces use visual composition, brand expression, and aesthetic harmony to create clarity, coherence, and emotional resonance. Unlike Assignment 1, which focused on usability and user needs, this assignment centers entirely on form, visual decision-making, and interface craftsmanship—the “look and feel” that communicates quality and brand credibility. Learning Objectives By the end of this assignment, you will be able to: Analyze interfaces using core aesthetic and compositional principles rather than usability metrics. Apply design vocabulary (layout, hierarchy, rhythm, proportion, balance, contrast, harmony) to describe visual systems precisely. Evaluate brand and visual consistency across interface elements. Reference theoretical frameworks such as Dieter Rams’ Ten Principles of Good Design, Gestalt principles, and neuroaesthetic clarity to justify your evaluations. Assignment Instructions Part 1 — Strong Design Aesthetics Identify three (3) professional websites that exemplify high-quality interface aesthetics and brand coherence. For each site, provide five (5) concrete visual examples (15 total). Each example must include a screenshot and a short analytical explanation (2–3 sentences) describing how specific visual choices achieve quality. Part 2 — Weak Design Aesthetics Select two (2) professional websites that fail to demonstrate strong aesthetic principles. Identify four (4) examples per site (8 total) with screenshots and analytic commentary. Describe why each violates aesthetic quality—e.g., visual clutter, inconsistent alignment, poor contrast, off-brand typography, overuse of gradients, or conflicting imagery. Maintain an objective, formal tone grounded in visual design terminology (avoid “I dislike this”; use “The lack of visual hierarchy flattens the composition and diminishes readability.”). Evaluation Framework Focus your observations on: Composition & Balance: How elements are distributed; visual rhythm and alignment. Hierarchy: How scale, contrast, and spatial grouping guide perception. Color Theory: How hue, saturation, and value create mood or emphasize brand voice. Typography: How font choice, weight, and spacing express tone and readability. Imagery & Iconography: Cohesion of visual language and photographic/illustrative style. Brand Consistency: Repetition of forms, color, and tone that reinforce identity. White Space & Proportion: How breathing room supports visual clarity and focus. Use the following principles (drawn from lectures and readings) to guide analysis: Balance & Harmony – Visual weight and rhythm create stability.
